Where New Jersey needs the most help

I combined four public measures into one need score for each county, then grouped the counties that look alike. A score of 50 is the New Jersey average. Middlesex, where I live, comes in a little under that, but it still has one of the biggest headcounts in the state.

How the score works

No black box here. Pick a county on the map and you can see exactly which numbers move its score.

What this can't tell you

  • It can't see inside a county. A town with high need can hide inside a county with a low score.
  • It doesn't count pets. No public data shows how many pets live in each county, so this measures people's need, not pets' need.
  • The four ingredients count equally. That was my choice, and a different choice would change the order a little.
  • The numbers are from different years (2019 to 2024), so it's a snapshot, not a live reading.
  • A score never decides who gets help. Everyone on this site sees the same listings.

How I made this

The need score averages four standardized measures: food insecurity (2023), poverty (2019 to 2023), the share of people with limited access to healthy food (2019), and unemployment (2024). The groups come from k-means clustering, checked against hierarchical clustering, which gave nearly the same groups.

Limits: county numbers hide big differences between towns, and no public data shows pet ownership by county.
