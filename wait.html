Which shelter pets wait the longest?

Zuzu waited at a shelter before I found her, and I kept wondering who waits longest. So I looked at about 75,000 real adoptions from the Austin Animal Center (2013 to 2024). Here's what the data says.

Compared with a similar pet, holding everything else equal (age, sex, health, how they arrived, and year). Lines show the 95% confidence range.

How to read this

Each bar compares two pets who are the same in every way the shelter recorded, except one thing. "+113%" for senior cats means a senior cat waited a bit more than twice as long as a young adult cat who was otherwise alike.

The thin line on each bar is the range the real answer most likely falls in. A short line means I can be more sure.

What this can't tell you

  • It ranks pets well but can't call the exact day. On pets it had never seen, flagged "slowest 20%" dogs waited 42 days versus 18 for the rest, yet only 42% of day estimates landed within a factor of two.
  • It's from one Texas shelter, so New Jersey shelters may differ. It only counts pets who were adopted.
  • It shows patterns, not causes. A senior cat doesn't wait longer because she is worse. Fewer people look for seniors.
  • It should never be used to decide which pets get a chance. I built it to show who needs extra attention.
  • The famous black cat bias is real here, but small next to age and breed.
